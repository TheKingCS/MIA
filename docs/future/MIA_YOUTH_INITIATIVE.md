# MIA Youth, Education & Human Development Initiative (parked)

**Status (2026-10-01): parked, by the owner's decision.** "Backburner,
potential future idea. Perfect MIA for personal use, but design it for
more than just me and my household, so it's ready if we go that way."

What that means in practice now (see `docs/ROADMAP.md`, "People and

## Handoff 1: MIA — Youth, Education & Human Development Initiative

1. **Purpose / new north star.** MIA began as a personal, local-first
   operating system. The larger purpose: a platform that helps people,
   especially young people, develop skills, organization, character,
   confidence, compassion and practical capability, and eventually
   free for students in schools. Not political or ideological
   indoctrination: human development, agency, responsibility,
   practical education, healthy relationships, empathy, purpose,
   service. A particularly important part: helping young men develop
   a healthier understanding of masculinity and relationships, framed
   as "You have enormous potential. Learn how to use it responsibly,"
   not "men are bad." A good man controls himself, respects boundaries
   and "no", understands consent, handles rejection without entitlement
   or retaliation, treats women as complete human beings, can admit
   when he's wrong, protects the vulnerable, builds rather than
   destroys, takes responsibility, uses strength to protect, makes
   people around him safer, develops useful skills, contributes.
2. **Three layers.** *MIA Personal* ("help me become capable": habits,
   routines, goals, tasks, projects, learning, finances, property,
   fitness, hobbies, journaling, life state, memory, missions,
   progression). *MIA Youth* ("help me become a good human": character,
   emotional intelligence, communication, healthy relationships,
   consent, boundaries, rejection, conflict, self-control, digital /
   social media / pornography / AI literacy, financial literacy,
   careers, trades, cooking, home and vehicle basics, organization,
   time management, purpose, leadership, service, environment,
   entrepreneurship), favoring interactive scenarios and practice over
   lectures. *MIA Community* ("help us make our community better":
   gardens, greenhouses, food drives, helping elderly residents, home
   repair, restoration, volunteering, events, mentorship, tech and
   trade projects, local entrepreneurship), so character education
   becomes action.
3. **Habits & routines** as a core module, not a checkbox tracker:
   integrated with life state, missions, progression, character and
   scheduling; categories from homework to service; pattern
   recognition without nagging ("You usually have trouble starting
   homework after practice. Want a 20-minute transition routine?");
   definitions, frequency, streaks, flexible schedules, stacking,
   templates, context-aware suggestions, analytics, goals, rewards.
   Healthy, sustainable patterns, not a compulsive productivity machine.
4. **Organizations & communities**, general-purpose: members, roles,
   goals, projects, tasks, events, resources, announcements,
   achievements, permissions, integrations; for schools, classes,
   clubs, teams, volunteer groups, gardens, faith groups, robotics,
   trade programs, mentorship, neighborhoods.
5. **School mode**: schools manage classes, clubs, teams, events,
   assignments, projects, announcements, resources, service
   opportunities; a student who joins "Greenhouse Club" sees its
   project, tasks, upcoming sessions and resources in their MIA.
6. **School portal**: verify the school, set policies, connect
   identity/information systems, import classes, set roles, invite
   students, give free access, manage school content. Integrate (Google
   Workspace for Education, Microsoft 365/Entra, Clever, ClassLink,
   Canvas, Schoology, SIS/LMS) rather than replace; build a clean
   connector architecture first, not the connectors.
7. **Privacy and permissions (critical).** School access must not equal
   access to a student's private MIA. Separate domains: student private
   (habits, goals, journal, personal projects, conversations,
   reminders); school institutional (classes, organizations, events,
   assignments, approved content, announcements); teacher (what they
   manage, appropriate participation); administrator (configuration,
   integrations, policies, aggregate analytics). Privacy in the data
   model from the start; stricter rules for minors (guardians, school
   authorization, retention, law). The school does not own all student
   data.
8. **Youth experience** that feels different from adult productivity
   software: character, relationships, masculinity (strength without
   domination, confidence without entitlement, sexuality without
   objectification, leadership without control, courage without
   cruelty), digital life, practical life, community. Not anti-boy.
9. **School presentation**: agency, not fear or shame. "Nobody is born
   knowing how to be a good man, woman, friend, spouse, parent, leader
   or human being. We learn it... The question isn't whether you're
   going to be taught. It's whether you're going to think about what
   you're being taught."
10. **Personal safety (separate, long-term R&D)**: user-controlled
    states (yellow: quietly tell trusted contacts; orange: share
    location, start preserving evidence; red: emergency response),
    possibly wearable. MIA must never decide on its own that someone is
    an attacker and accuse or report them; designed around consent,
    privacy, false positives, evidence integrity, due process, law.
11. **Architecture**: MIA → Person (habits, goals, life state → missions
    → progression → skills → character → service) and Organization
    (school, club, community). MIA helps individuals, groups and the
    institutions that serve people.
12. **Approach**: audit first; extend existing primitives (users,
    identity, roles, missions, progression, skills, life state, tasks,
    projects, events, notifications, memory, AI orchestration,
    registries, security, UI); don't duplicate.
13. **Testing**: permissions, role boundaries, privacy, membership,
    student/teacher/admin separation, habits, organization tasks and
    events, integration boundaries, data ownership, minors' privacy,
    notifications, failures; above all, no unauthorized school access
    to personal student data.
14. **Philosophy**: never "an AI that tells young people what to think";
    a tool that helps them learn how to think. Agency, curiosity,
    responsibility, compassion, critical thinking, self-control,
    service, competence, healthy relationships, community; capable
    humans, not dependent users.
15. **Long-term**: free software → free education → practical skills →
    mentorship → community projects → material resources → stronger
    communities; a private portfolio of real accomplishments. "Build
    better humans. Build better communities. Give people the tools to
    build better lives." Without compromising local-first privacy; the
    personal system stays the foundation.

## Handoff 2: MIA Youth — Gender-Inclusive Human Development

1. The program is not primarily about boys, masculinity or preventing
   sexual violence; those are one important pathway. Central purpose:
   help young people become capable, confident, compassionate,
   responsible human beings, equally relevant to boys and girls.
2. Core message: "You are becoming a person. You get to decide what
   kind of person you become, and you deserve the tools to do that
   well." Agency and development, not shame.
3. Three domains: **Self** (confidence, habits, skills, purpose,
   health, identity), **Others** (relationships, consent, boundaries,
   communication, empathy, conflict), **World** (community, service,
   environment, leadership, building, stewardship).
4. Girls should feel MIA was built for them too: not "how to avoid
   dangerous men" (that puts the burden on potential victims) but "you
   deserve to be safe, respected, capable and free." Self-respect (your
   boundaries matter; you can say no, change your mind, leave, ask for
   help; you don't owe attention, affection or sex). Praise capability,
   not mainly appearance ("you solved something difficult, built
   something, helped someone, stood up for yourself").
5. Relationships without losing yourself, for everyone: healthy
   attachment, independence, friendships, jealousy, controlling
   behavior, manipulation, love-bombing, isolation, breakups, keeping
   your own goals. "You can love someone without surrendering your
   identity to them," and "you can love someone and still leave them."
6. Personal safety without victim-blaming: tools offered as optional
   resources, never obligations; recognizing uncomfortable situations,
   leaving, trusted adults, digital privacy, stalking, harassment,
   coercion, helping a friend, evidence, professional resources.
7. Girls encouraged to develop strength and opportunity: trades,
   building, cars, code, technology, lifting, outdoors, business,
   financial independence, science, art, leadership; no artificial
   limits.
8. "Healthy Strength": compassionate without being passive, feminine
   without being weak, ambitious without being selfish, independent
   without being isolated, nurturing without being responsible for
   everyone, forgiving without giving continued access to hurt you.
9. Healthy masculinity as one specialized pathway: "You have enormous
   potential. Learn how to use it responsibly." (strength without
   domination ... becoming trustworthy; doesn't need to humiliate
   someone to feel powerful).
10. Everyone learns the same human-to-human principles: no means respect
    it; yes isn't yes forever; changing your mind is respected; check in
    when someone looks uncomfortable; don't exploit vulnerability or
    weaponize trust; don't retaliate against rejection; don't publicly
    humiliate; don't spread what's told privately; don't join the crowd
    against someone.
11. Consent is universal: your boundaries and others', asking,
    uncertainty, rejection, withdrawal, intoxication, pressure and
    coercion, consent beyond sex. "Your body belongs to you, and other
    people's bodies belong to them."
12. Both sides of a boundary always together: you may have boundaries,
    and you're responsible for respecting others'.
13. Signature theme: "What kind of person do you want to be when nobody
    is watching?" Trustworthy, kind, courageous, self-controlled, able
    to admit wrong, helping, respecting boundaries, standing up for
    people not in the room, building something useful, caring for
    yourself and others without controlling them.
14. Avoid rigid gender boxes; gender-specific pathways exist because
    some pressures differ, but everyone gets the full curriculum.
15. MIA's role: "Here is information, perspectives, tools, questions;
    let's think about this together," not "this is what you must
    believe"; clear about fundamental safety and consent principles.
16. Goal: capable, compassionate, confident, responsible, curious,
    resilient, self-aware, useful, trustworthy, independent,
    community-minded young people, not obedient students or dependent
    AI users.
17. MIA Youth is a free human-development and life-skills platform.
    The question it helps answer: "What kind of person do I want to
    become, and what can I do today to move toward that person?"

## Claude's review (2026-10-01), for when this is picked back up

**What fits already:** Missions, Skills, progression, Classroom and the
textbook tutor (curriculum and portfolio bones); recurring routines
with streaks (a habit seed); projects, tasks, events and builds (a club
project is a build with a parts list); and the behavior principles
("you never decide for them", facts from records rather than the
model's opinion, the communication gate, the deterministic safety
floor), which match "help them learn how to think, not what to think."

**Gaps and risks found:**
1. No real per-person data model: most stores are household-wide; only
   missions and skills are per profile. (Being fixed now, see
   ROADMAP "People and ownership"; it's needed for any multi-person use.)
2. Local-first vs. school-wide: recommended shape is "a student's MIA
   is theirs, on their device; a school or club is something they join
   and get a feed from"; information flows to the student by default
   and back only when the student shares.
3. Platform: students use Chromebooks and phones; the PySide6 desktop
   doesn't run there. Youth would be web/phone-first.
4. Minors: COPPA, FERPA, guardian consent, retention; and the hardest
   design question, a private journal / "talk it out" companion vs. a
   school's duty to report. Needs counselors and legal input.
5. A small local model must not author consent/relationship teaching:
   lessons and scenarios should be human-written and expert-reviewed;
   the model facilitates practice and reflection.
6. Adult design choices to reverse for youth: maximal logging → minimum
   collection; XP/streaks → avoid compulsion; warm companion → coach,
   to avoid dependence.
7. Scope: test what exists for real before expanding.
