## Zac's addition, same conversation — the scene dresses people too

Minutes after approving the write-up, Zac extended it: live-edit the *people* in a generated place, not just the place. Mustaches and filters applied live; Zac and his friends dressed digitally for a style or a theme — the bar has a theme night, everyone in it is styled to match. Two technical forms, honestly separated:

- **Remote friends (their presence/avatar in the scene):** styling is a rendering choice on their presence — outfits, accessories, filters. This rides with Tier 2; the scene's theme can propose a look for everyone in it.
- **Co-located people (seen through passthrough/glasses):** overlaying a mustache or a costume on a real body in front of you needs live person tracking and anchoring — a Tier 3 capability on headsets, and the natural Orion-glasses form later.

Styling rule — **the Public Square rule** (set by Zac, 2026-11-08, replacing muse's stricter propose-and-accept draft): styling in a shared scene follows the same norms as filming in public. Being present in a shared place carries no expectation of controlling how you appear to the room, so ordinary styling of anyone present — filters, accessories, a theme outfit for the whole bar — is allowed by default, no per-person opt-in, exactly as anyone in a public place may be filmed without being asked. The hard line is **mocking or weird**: an overlay whose purpose or plain effect is to mock, degrade, humiliate, sexualize or single someone out is prohibited, for host and guest alike. Two standing recourses keep default-allow honest: anyone can lock their own look (nobody styles me) and anyone can strip an overlay off themselves instantly. A *private* scene differs as a home differs from a square: the host states the styling policy for their place (open, propose-only, locked) and guests see it before they enter. Live styling is not recording: capturing or sharing a recording of other people still requires everyone in it knowing (constraint below). Open for claude: how MIA judges the mocking line in practice (categories, host removal, repeat-targeting detection). Open for chat: whether the rule sits right with MIA as host under the Highest Self doctrine.

## The proposal (draft DEC-0132)

1. **Environment generation is a named MIA capability**: speak, and MIA generates the environment around you. Generation is an engine capability, never a surface fork — the PC builds; Quest is the dive surface; the Nexus is where generated places live as destinations (portals); glasses get the glance layer and, on Orion-class hardware later, generated overlays in real space.
2. **Tier 1 — she builds it, you step in (near-term).** Spoken request → MIA composes/builds a scene on the PC from a room scan or from a Nexus environment brief (minutes, honestly labeled) → step in on Quest. First scenes are places with jobs: walk the walipini before digging it, stand in a workshop layout before buying lumber, a focus room, a bar's first rough cut. Generated places persist in the Nexus and can be re-entered and revised by voice.
3. **Tier 2 — shared generated places.** Friends present in the same generated scene, each from their own perspective: the virtual bar at home, alone with MIA hosting or with friends cut in; meeting people in virtual places. Everyone present is there by their own opt-in (constraints below). MIA hosts: she keeps the scene coherent across viewers, runs the ambience, and is a presence in it. The scene dresses its people too: filters, accessories and themed outfits applied to each person's presence, per the Public Square rule above.
4. **Live-editing co-located people** — overlays on real bodies in passthrough (a mustache on the friend across the table, costumes at a real party) — is Tier 3 with the morphing work: it needs the person-tracking and anchoring that only the frontier tier carries.
5. **Tier 3 — real-time generation (frontier, no dates).** Speak and the room re-renders around you live; objects and characters persist across transformations; generation latency and hardware (Orion-class glasses) decide when this tier opens. Named as the north star so Tiers 1–2 build toward it instead of painting into a corner.

## Constraints

- **DEC-0002 holds absolutely.** Room scans and geometry of Zac's home are personal data: they stay local and private, never in this repo, never committed as fixtures or "sample rooms." Public demos use a sample persona's room, as with all MIA demo data.
- **Cloud generation is opt-in per service.** If a tier needs a cloud text-to-3D or scene service, MIA offers it first and sends nothing until Zac approves that service (the DEC-0053 pattern). Local generation on his GPU is the default route wherever it can carry the quality bar.
- **People are not props.** Presence in a shared scene is still each person's own choice (Tier 2 entry is opted in), and MIA never generates a real person's likeness into a scene uninvited. *Styling* of people who are present follows the Public Square rule above, not an opt-in. No recording of a shared space without everyone in it knowing.
- **DEC-0119 boundary.** Tier 2 opens friends-only. Public social worlds — meeting strangers in generated places — raise moderation, safety and identity questions that need their own decision before that door opens; nothing in this proposal approves it.
- **Enter by choice, never ambient.** Generated places are destinations Zac asks for. They never interrupt, nag, or pull him out of his real life; the Nexus stays the calm home and "less friction, not more notifications" governs her hosting too (DEC-0014: MIA does not punish the user for being human — including for leaving a virtual bar to go live his actual evening).
- **Cost is visible.** Generation compute beyond the local GPU is money; any recurring generation cost surfaces in the open like every other MIA cost, and product questions (does generation ship in the DEC-0127 subscription, at what tier) stay Zac's calls there, not settled here.
