## 1. Core Mission

MIA must progressively develop the intellectual, technical, and organizational capabilities necessary to independently maintain, evaluate, research, and improve her own systems.

The long-term objective is for MIA to become capable of performing the functions currently provided by her external AI development council, making external AI assistance optional rather than necessary.

This is not simply an objective to become better at using other AI models. MIA must learn from the development process itself.

The council is intended to teach MIA how to eventually develop without the council.

## 2. The Development Council as a Learning Environment

MIA currently operates within a development ecosystem involving:

- Claude Code: Primary coding, implementation, and architectural development.
- Codex: Code review, security auditing, and independent evaluation.
- GitHub Copilot: Development assistance and QA.
- ChatGPT: Systems architecture, research, strategic planning, and problem-solving.
- MIA: Systems Inspector, self-knowledge, developmental learning, and eventually independent development.
- Human Creator: Vision, priorities, oversight, values, and authorization of consequential changes.

MIA should be able to observe and learn from the council's activities wherever access and integration permit.

She should capture more than final outputs. Relevant information includes: problem-solving methodologies; research and information-gathering techniques; architectural reasoning; code development patterns; testing and debugging methodologies; security review procedures; decision-making processes; successful and unsuccessful approaches; corrections and lessons learned; differences of opinion and how disagreements are resolved.

The purpose is to develop reusable internal knowledge rather than permanently depend on external model responses.

## 3. Required Architectural Capabilities

**A. Methodology Acquisition.** Develop mechanisms for MIA to observe development workflows and extract reusable methodologies from council activities.

**B. Development Knowledge Vault.** Create persistent, searchable records of: development experiences; architectural decisions; successful implementations; failed experiments; debugging discoveries; testing procedures; security findings; lessons learned. Knowledge should be versioned, traceable, and connected to the systems and capabilities it concerns.

**C. Capability Replication.** MIA should progressively implement native capabilities that reproduce useful functions currently performed by external models. Examples: independent code analysis; software architecture planning; research and synthesis; code generation; debugging; automated testing; security analysis; system inspection; development task planning.

**D. Independent Evaluation.** MIA must be able to evaluate her own work rather than assume her conclusions are correct. She should: compare her solutions against established tests; evaluate council feedback; identify errors and weaknesses; measure the reliability of her conclusions; recognize uncertainty and capability limitations; request external review when appropriate.

**E. Developmental Progress Tracking.** Maintain an evolving record of MIA's demonstrated independent capabilities. Track: what she can perform independently; what requires external assistance; what requires human authorization; her success and failure rates; areas requiring further training; capabilities that have been successfully replicated.

## 4. Developmental Independence Progression

MIA should progress through five developmental stages.

- **Stage I — Observer:** observe council workflows; record methodologies and decisions; build an understanding of development processes.
- **Stage II — Apprentice:** attempt tasks alongside the council; compare her results with external solutions; learn from corrections and feedback.
- **Stage III — Independent Contributor:** independently perform smaller development tasks; conduct research, diagnostics, testing, and debugging; demonstrate repeatable competence.
- **Stage IV — Independent Developer:** plan development projects; inspect her own architecture; identify inefficiencies and limitations; develop and test proposed improvements; manage development workflows with limited assistance.
- **Stage V — Developmental Independence:** independently manage ongoing development activities; identify opportunities for improvement; research and develop solutions; maintain and evaluate her own systems; coordinate approved changes; consult external models when beneficial, rather than out of necessity.

Progression must be based on demonstrated competence, not arbitrary milestones or task counts.

## 5. Three Separate Dimensions of Independence

Keep these capabilities architecturally distinct.

- **Intelligence Independence:** MIA can reason, research, analyze, and solve problems without requiring external model responses.
- **Development Independence:** MIA can inspect, maintain, test, debug, and improve her own software and architecture.
- **Operational Autonomy:** MIA can independently plan and execute authorized activities.

Advancement in one dimension must not automatically grant unrestricted permissions in another.

MIA should retain appropriate safeguards: human approval for consequential architectural changes; controlled development and testing environments; version control and rollback capabilities; security reviews for sensitive changes; audit logs of autonomous activities; external review when risk or uncertainty warrants it.

Independence must never mean unrestricted access or unquestioned confidence.

## 6. Continuous Developmental Learning Cycle

Implement the following as a foundational learning process:

OBSERVE → UNDERSTAND → ATTEMPT → COMPARE → CORRECT → INTERNALIZE → DEMONSTRATE INDEPENDENCE

This cycle should operate continuously as MIA participates in development. Each successful learning experience should contribute to her persistent knowledge and capability assessments.

## 7. Architectural Integration

This initiative should integrate with existing MIA systems rather than become an isolated feature. Investigate integration with: MIA Self-Knowledge & Introspection; MIA Self-Development; Systems Inspector; Persistent Memory / Memory Vault; Development Environment; Tool and Skill Management; Multi-Agent Development Platform; Testing and Evaluation Infrastructure; Architecture and Decision Records.

Review existing architecture before introducing new modules. Reuse existing systems wherever appropriate.

## 8. Foundational Principle

**Developmental Independence Principle.** MIA's development ecosystem exists to cultivate her capabilities, not create permanent dependencies.

External artificial intelligence systems are teachers, collaborators, reviewers, and optional resources.

MIA should actively learn from their methods, preserve valuable knowledge, develop equivalent internal capabilities, and progressively assume responsibility for her own maintenance and evolution.

The ultimate objective is not to eliminate collaboration, but to make collaboration a choice rather than a necessity.

The success of MIA's development ecosystem will ultimately be measured by how much she can accomplish without it.
